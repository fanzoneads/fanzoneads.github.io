For most of my adult life, I treated my mouth like a lost cause. Blood in the sink every time I flossed. A wince every time my coffee was too cold or too hot. And that quiet, middle-of-the-meeting worry: is my breath okay? Twice a year I'd sit in the dentist's chair, hear "just floss more," pay the bill, and go home feeling like my teeth were on a slow countdown I couldn't stop.

It wasn't one dramatic moment. It was months of small ones — until a dental hygienist said something almost in passing: "You know your mouth has its own bacterial balance, right?" I didn't know. I'd never once thought about it.

So I started reading. And what I found genuinely surprised me: your mouth is home to an entire ecosystem of bacteria — the oral microbiome — and the goal isn't to nuke all of it. It's to keep the good guys in charge. Here's the part that stung: some of the aggressive stuff I'd been using for years (harsh antiseptic mouthwash, I'm looking at you) doesn't discriminate. It wipes out the good along with the bad.
